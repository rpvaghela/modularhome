What We Handle

Our scope typically includes the modular building process from factory production through module installation onsite.

This commonly includes:

In-Factory Construction

The modules are constructed inside controlled production environments where major portions of the building are fabricated before delivery.

Depending on project scope, this may include:

  • structural framing

  • sheathing systems

  • wall systems

  • floor systems

  • roofing systems

  • rough-ins

  • windows and exterior components

  • interior preparation

Factory construction allows for:

  • controlled quality environments

  • reduced weather exposure

  • predictable production scheduling

  • improved workflow efficiency

Third-Party Inspections

Commercial modular projects are engineered and inspected to comply with required state and local building codes.

Throughout production, projects may go through:

  • third-party inspections

  • quality-control checkpoints

  • engineering reviews

  • structural compliance inspections

This helps ensure modules are built according to approved plans and applicable code requirements.

Transportation Coordination

Once production is complete, the modules must be carefully transported to the jobsite.

Our role often includes:

  • transportation logistics coordination

  • delivery scheduling

  • route planning

  • oversized load coordination

  • shipment sequencing

Commercial modular transportation requires extensive planning to ensure:

  • safe delivery

  • proper scheduling

  • efficient onsite installation timing

Crane and Set Operations

One of the most critical phases of modular construction is:

the set.

Once modules arrive onsite, they are:

  • craned into position

  • structurally connected

  • aligned according to engineered plans

We coordinate crane and set operations to help ensure the installation process moves efficiently and safely.

This phase dramatically accelerates visible project progress because large sections of the building arrive pre-assembled.

What the General Contractor Typically Handles

While we focus on fabrication and module delivery, the General Contractor (GC) typically manages the traditional site-development portions of the project.

This often includes:

Site Work

  • grading

  • excavation

  • drainage

  • land preparation

Foundations

  • slabs

  • footings

  • crawlspaces

  • structural foundations

Utility Infrastructure

  • sewer

  • water

  • electrical

  • gas

  • storm systems

Final Site Completion

  • utility tie-ins

  • exterior completion

  • sidewalks

  • paving

  • landscaping

  • final finishes

  • occupancy coordination

The General Contractor also typically oversees:

  • local subcontractors

  • permit coordination

  • onsite project management

  • final inspections

Modular Construction Is a Collaborative Process

Commercial modular construction works best when:

  • developers

  • modular manufacturers

  • engineers

  • architects

  • general contractors

all work together early in the planning process.

Coordination is extremely important because modular projects involve:

  • factory production schedules

  • transportation logistics

  • crane planning

  • foundation timing

  • utility preparation

The earlier the collaboration begins, the smoother the project execution typically becomes.

Why Developers Like This Approach

Many developers prefer this structure because it allows:

  • factory production efficiency

  • faster building timelines

  • parallel construction processes

  • scalable project delivery

While site crews prepare the property:

  • the building itself is simultaneously being fabricated.

That schedule compression is one of the biggest reasons modular construction is growing so rapidly nationwide.

Why Modular Construction Accelerates Commercial Projects

Traditional construction often forces developers to wait through:

  • weather delays

  • framing schedules

  • subcontractor bottlenecks

  • material exposure

  • labor shortages

Modular construction changes this process by allowing:

  • site work

  • factory construction

to happen simultaneously.

That can dramatically reduce overall project timelines.

  • engineered

just like traditional construction.

how the building is produced and delivered.

across the country.
