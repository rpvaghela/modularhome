Who Invented the ZIP System?

ZIP System was developed by:

Huber Engineered Woods

which is part of:

J.M. Huber Corporation,

a long-established American manufacturing company founded in the 1800s. 

Huber developed ZIP System as a way to simplify and modernize:

  • wall sheathing

  • weather protection

  • exterior drying-in processes

for builders.

What Problem Was ZIP Trying to Solve?

Before ZIP System became popular,
builders traditionally had to install:

  1. wall sheathing
    THEN

  2. separate house wrap

This process created several problems:

  • slower installation

  • torn house wrap

  • moisture exposure

  • weather delays

  • wind damage during construction

  • installation mistakes

Builders increasingly wanted:

a faster and cleaner system.

The BIG Idea Behind ZIP System

The idea was simple but revolutionary:

combine the sheathing AND weather barrier into ONE product.

Instead of:

  • installing OSB sheathing
    THEN

  • wrapping the house afterward

ZIP combined both into:

one integrated panel system.

That dramatically simplified the:

  • dry-in process

  • wall assembly process

  • weather protection workflow

What Makes ZIP Different?

ZIP panels are basically:

structural wall sheathing panels

with:

  • built-in water-resistant barriers

already attached to the panel surface.

The seams are then sealed using:

ZIP tape

creating a weather-resistant building envelope. 

Why Builders LOVED It Immediately

Builders quickly realized ZIP helped:

  • speed up projects

  • reduce labor

  • improve organization

  • simplify construction sequencing

The ability to:

dry in homes faster

became a MASSIVE advantage.

What Does “Dry In” Mean Again?

“Drying in” means protecting the home from weather using:

  • roof systems

  • sheathing

  • weather barriers

  • windows

  • exterior doors

The faster a structure gets dried in,
the less risk there is of:

  • rain damage

  • moisture exposure

  • warped materials

  • delays

This is one reason ZIP exploded in popularity.
