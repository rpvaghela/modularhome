What Is a Modular Home? The Complete Guide to Modular Homes, Prefab Homes, and Factory-Built Housing

Source: https://modularhome.com/blogs/modular-construction/what-is-a-modular-home-the-complete-guide-to-modular-homes-prefab-homes-and-factory-built-housing

A modular home is a real residential home that is built in large sections inside a factory-controlled environment and then transported to a property where it is assembled on a permanent foundation. Unlike a traditional site-built home, which is constructed almost entirely outdoors at the jobsite, a modular home is built off-site in a manufacturing facility using a streamlined construction process.

Modular homes are also commonly called prefab homes, factory-built homes, system-built homes, or off-site constructed homes. While those terms are sometimes used interchangeably, the most important thing to understand is this: a modular home is not the same thing as a mobile home or manufactured home.

A true modular home is usually built to local and state residential building codes, placed on a permanent foundation, and designed to look, feel, and function like a traditional house.

What Does “Modular Home” Mean?

The word “modular” refers to the way the home is built.

Instead of building the entire house piece by piece at the property, a modular home is constructed in sections, also called modules. These modules are built inside a factory and then delivered to the building site.

Once the modules arrive, they are lifted into place, connected together, sealed, finished, and completed on the foundation.

A modular home may arrive in:

  • One large section

  • Two sections

  • Multiple modules

  • Several building components

  • Panelized or hybrid systems depending on the design

The final result can be a small cabin, a ranch-style home, a two-story house, a modern prefab home, an ADU, a vacation cabin, or even a large custom modular home.

Modular Home vs Manufactured Home

One of the most important distinctions in factory-built housing is the difference between a modular home and a manufactured home.

A modular home is typically built to residential building codes and installed on a permanent foundation.

A manufactured home is built to federal HUD code and is often associated with mobile homes or homes designed to be transported on a chassis.

This difference matters because it can affect:

  • Financing

  • Appraisal

  • Insurance

  • Zoning

  • Resale value

  • Foundation requirements

  • Local code approval

Many buyers search for “modular home vs manufactured home” because the two terms are often confused. But they are not the same type of housing.

Modular Home vs Mobile Home

A modular home is also not the same thing as a mobile home.

Mobile homes were historically built before modern manufactured housing standards and were designed to be moved. Modular homes are generally placed on permanent foundations and are intended to remain in one location like a traditional house.

A modular home can look like:

  • A modern ranch home

  • A farmhouse

  • A steel-framed home

  • A mountain retreat

  • A lake house

The design possibilities are much broader than many people realize.

Modular Home vs Prefab Home

The terms “modular home” and “prefab home” are closely related.

Prefab home is a broader term that means prefabricated home. It can refer to any home where significant parts are built off-site before being transported to the final property.

Prefab homes may include:

  • Modular homes

  • Panelized homes

  • SIP panel homes

  • Steel house kits

  • Precut kits

  • Tiny homes

  • Cabin kits

  • ADUs

  • Hybrid building systems

A modular home is one specific type of prefab home.

So all modular homes are prefab homes, but not all prefab homes are modular homes.

How Are Modular Homes Built?

The modular home building process usually begins with design and planning.

A buyer chooses a floor plan, layout, exterior style, and finish package. The project is then reviewed for site conditions, local code requirements, transportation access, foundation needs, and engineering requirements.

Once approved, the home is built inside a factory.

Factory construction may include:

  • Floor framing

  • Wall framing

  • Roof framing

  • Windows

  • Doors

  • Electrical rough-in

  • Plumbing rough-in

  • Insulation

  • Drywall

  • Cabinetry

  • Flooring

  • Interior finishes

  • Exterior materials

While the home is being built in the factory, site work can often happen at the same time. This is one of the biggest advantages of modular construction.

The property may be prepared with:

  • Foundation work

  • Utility installation

  • Driveway access

  • Excavation

  • Grading

  • Septic or sewer planning

  • Water connections

  • Crane access

When the modules are ready, they are transported to the property and installed on the foundation.

Why Are Modular Homes Faster to Build?

Modular homes are often faster because the factory and jobsite work can happen at the same time.

In traditional construction, many steps happen one after another. The foundation is prepared, then framing begins, then the home is dried in, then trades come in, then finishes begin.

With modular construction, the home can be built in the factory while the land is being prepared.

This parallel process can reduce delays and improve scheduling.

Modular construction can also reduce weather-related slowdowns because much of the home is built indoors. Rain, snow, wind, mud, and extreme temperatures can create major delays for traditional site-built homes. Factory construction helps reduce exposure to those conditions.

The biggest financial advantage is often predictability. Factory construction can reduce some of the unexpected delays and inefficiencies common in traditional construction.

What Is a Turnkey Modular Home?

A turnkey modular home is a home package designed to be mostly complete and move-in ready after installation, site connections, and final inspections.

A turnkey package may include more of the total project scope, such as:

  • Home construction

  • Delivery

  • Plumbing connections

  • Electrical connections

  • Final trim

  • Completion work

However, “turnkey” can mean different things depending on the company. Some builders include foundation, utilities, and permits. Others do not.

Buyers should always confirm what turnkey means in writing.

What Is a Dried-In Modular Home or Shell?

A dried-in modular home or shell usually means the structure is installed and protected from the weather, but not fully finished inside.

A dried-in package may include:

  • Framing

  • Roofing

  • Exterior walls

  • Windows

  • Exterior doors

  • Weather barrier

  • Basic structural assembly

It may not include:

  • Final plumbing

  • Final electrical

  • HVAC

  • Flooring

  • Cabinets

  • Interior trim

  • Paint

  • Appliances

  • Site utilities

  • Permits

  • Foundation

Dried-in shell packages can be attractive for buyers who want to finish the interior themselves or hire local contractors.

What Happens on Modular Home Set Day?

Set day is when the modular home sections are placed on the foundation.

A crane or specialized lifting equipment is used to lift each module into position. Crews then align, connect, and secure the sections together.

Set day may include:

  • Module placement

  • Structural connections

  • Roof completion

  • Weather sealing

  • Exterior connections

  • Interior marriage line finishing

  • Temporary protection

  • Final installation steps

For many buyers, this is the most exciting day of the project because the home can appear on the foundation very quickly.

What Is the Difference Between Modular and Panelized Construction?

Modular construction uses large three-dimensional sections built in a factory.

Panelized construction uses wall panels, roof panels, floor panels, or structural panels that are built off-site and assembled at the property.

Panelized systems may include:

  • SIP panels

  • Wall panels

  • Steel panels

  • Pre-framed walls

  • Roof trusses

  • Floor systems

Panelized homes may require more on-site labor than modular homes, but they can offer more flexibility for certain designs or hard-to-access locations.

What Are SIP Panel Homes?

SIP stands for Structural Insulated Panel.

SIP panel homes use insulated panels for walls, roofs, or floors. These panels can create strong, energy-efficient building envelopes.

SIP homes are often associated with:

  • Energy efficiency

  • Fast shell assembly

  • Strong insulation performance

  • Tight building envelopes

  • Modern prefab construction

SIP panel homes are not always modular homes, but they are part of the broader prefab housing category.
