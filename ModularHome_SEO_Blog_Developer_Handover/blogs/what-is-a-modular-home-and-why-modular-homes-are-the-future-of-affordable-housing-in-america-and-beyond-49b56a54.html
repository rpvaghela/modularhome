What Is a Modular Home?

A modular home is a home built in sections inside a factory-controlled environment and then transported to the building site for final installation on a permanent foundation.

Unlike traditional site-built homes that are constructed entirely outdoors, modular homes are built indoors using precision engineering and modern manufacturing techniques.

The completed sections are delivered to the property and assembled into a finished home.

Modern modular homes can include:

  • Open floor plans

  • Luxury kitchens

  • Vaulted ceilings

  • Large porches

  • Attached garages

  • Smart home technology

  • Energy-efficient systems

  • Custom finishes

Most people cannot tell the difference between a modular home and a traditional site-built home once construction is complete.

Are Modular Homes the Same as Manufactured Homes?

No.

This is one of the biggest misconceptions in housing.

Modular Homes

  • Built to IRC and local building codes

  • Installed on permanent foundations

  • Appraise similarly to site-built homes

  • Often financed with traditional mortgages

  • Can appreciate in value

Manufactured Homes

  • Built to federal HUD standards

  • Constructed on permanent steel chassis

  • Often delivered as complete units

  • Different financing requirements

  • Different zoning requirements

Modular homes are generally considered a form of traditional real estate construction.

Why Are Modular Homes More Affordable?

Factory Efficiency

Traditional homes are built one house at a time.

Modular homes are built using streamlined manufacturing processes that allow builders to purchase materials more efficiently and reduce waste.

This often lowers overall construction costs.

Reduced Labor Costs

Labor is one of the largest expenses in home construction.

Factory-built housing allows builders to complete many construction tasks more efficiently than traditional site-built methods.

This helps reduce labor-related costs.

Less Material Waste

Construction waste is a major cost driver in traditional building.

Factory environments allow builders to optimize materials and reduce waste.

This improves overall efficiency and affordability.

Fewer Weather Delays

Rain, snow, wind, and extreme temperatures can delay traditional construction for weeks or months.

Modular homes are built indoors, allowing construction to continue year-round.

This reduces costly delays.
